## 4. Regulatory and Legal Risk — A Material Long-Term Overhang for Both

**Alphabet faces the more existential legal risk.** Its core Search business was found to be an illegal monopoly, and remedies are now live: 

the remedies in the government's search monopoly case against Google took effect in June 2026, ending exclusive default deals while leaving Chrome intact and the hardest questions on appeal

. The structural risk is not fully resolved — 

Google filed its opening brief at the D.C. Circuit on May 22, 2026, asking the court to overturn the underlying liability finding, while the DOJ and 38 states cross-appealed, pressing again for structural relief including Chrome divestiture, with oral argument expected in late 2026 or early 2027 and a final decision possibly not arriving until 2028

. While Google avoided the harshest outcomes (forced Chrome/Android divestiture), the litigation overhang will persist through most of the five-year window.

**Microsoft faces a growing but less advanced regulatory threat**, centered on cloud/software bundling rather than a core monopoly finding. 

The current FTC probe examines whether Microsoft uses its dominant position in productivity software to make switching away from Azure harder, costlier, or technically impractical, investigating licensing terms, interoperability barriers, and bundling across cloud computing, software licensing, cybersecurity, and AI products including Copilot

. In the UK, 

the CMA accepted nonbinding commitments from AWS and Microsoft to reduce or remove data egress fees and improve interoperability

, while 

launching a fresh investigation into Microsoft's licensing of products like Windows Server and SQL Server, focused on whether this steers customers toward Azure

. This is a real but more contained risk than Alphabet's — it concerns licensing practices, not the legality of Microsoft's core products.

**Bottom line:** Both companies enter the next five years as extraordinarily well-resourced, AI-era compounders with wide moats, but their resilience profiles differ in character. Microsoft's resilience is primarily **balance-sheet and cash-flow driven** — it is self-funding an unprecedented AI buildout without touching capital markets, carries the highest possible credit rating, and has a more diversified, enterprise-sticky revenue base, though it carries bundling/antitrust scrutiny and a degree of reliance on the evolving OpenAI relationship. Alphabet's resilience is more **structural and technological** — a deeply entrenched, network-effect-driven consumer franchise (Search, YouTube, Android, Chrome) combined with vertically integrated AI infrastructure (TPUs) and a cloud business now growing faster than peers — but it is taking on more financial leverage to fund growth and carries a heavier, adjudicated legal overhang around its core Search monopoly that will likely remain unresolved for several more years. Over a five-year horizon, both appear highly likely to remain dominant, cash-generative franchises; Microsoft screens as the more conservative, financially resilient holding, while Alphabet screens as the higher-growth, somewhat higher-risk position given its capex-funding shift and unresolved antitrust remedies.
